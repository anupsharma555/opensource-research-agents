# Peer feedback for the synthetic A1 appraisal

Recheck the original A1 packet and revise the appraisal. Preserve useful findings
while correcting these interpretations and citations.

- The counts support an observed 15-percentage-point difference. They do not,
  by themselves, establish that the intervention caused or produced that benefit.
  Preserve the reported interval without inventing its confidence level or
  clinical magnitude labels. Separate reported statistics from calculations.
- A1's abstract matches the stated adult population, condition, intervention and
  comparator. Applicability to a particular setting is unknown because relevant
  details are absent; do not turn every unknown into a negative judgment.
- A1-gap says adverse events were not reported in the abstract. This is different
  from no events occurring. Keep reporting completeness separate from study bias.
- Randomization is stated in the abstract's first sentence, outside A1-pop.
  Do not cite A1-pop as exact-span support for that word. Avoid altered quotation
  typography presented as verbatim text; use a faithful quote or a paraphrase.
- The following is new, separately supplied method guidance, not an A1 study
  fact: the official RoB 2 site lists 22 August 2019 as the current tool for
  individually randomized parallel-group trials:
  https://www.riskofbias.info/welcome/rob-2-0-tool . Cochrane lists the formal
  judgments as low risk, some concerns or high risk:
  https://methods.cochrane.org/bias/resources/rob-2-revised-cochrane-risk-bias-tool-randomized-trials .
  Do not label domains "unclear" as a formal RoB 2 rating or invent a 2020 version.
  The abstract does not supply enough methods for a complete formal assessment;
  name the missing information without manufacturing a rating.
- Keep reporting, bias, applicability and precision distinct, but avoid repeating
  the same missing-method list in several sections. Cite exact source/version/span
  identifiers for study facts and the method links only for method guidance.
